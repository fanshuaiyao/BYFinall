                                          Abstract

      Representation learning tailored for molecular property prediction aims to project complex
molecular structures and features into high-dimensional mathematical representations via deep
learning techniques. These representations are subsequently utilized to accurately predict various
biochemical properties, ultimately facilitating the efficient screening of target molecules with
therapeutic potential from vast pools of drug candidates. Although the pharmaceutical domain
has accumulated massive volumes of molecular data, such data are often plagued by severe noise
and inconsistent annotation quality. Concurrently, traditional computational methods face
prohibitive computational complexity when processing large-scale molecular datasets. In recent
years, the rapid advancement of deep learning in data representation has provided a novel,
data-driven paradigm for molecular information modeling. However, effectively leveraging
multimodal molecular data to construct comprehensive and robust molecular feature
representations within a deep learning framework--thereby achieving high-precision predictions
of biochemical properties--remains a core and pressing challenge in this field.

      To address the aforementioned challenges, leveraging the multimodal nature of molecular
representations, this thesis proposes two innovative methods:

      (1) A molecular property prediction method based on Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
enhancement and message passing. To tackle the interpretability issues of Large Language
Models (LLMs) lacking pharmacological logic support when predicting complex biochemical
properties, as well as the limitations of traditional Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) regarding
restricted topological perception and information backflow, this thesis proposes an end-to-end
dual-tower prediction framework. In the text encoder, the LLaMA-3.1 large language model and
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique are introduced. Combined with Chain-of-Thought
prompting, this guides the model to spontaneously generate explicit logical reasoning,
transforming the inference process into interpretable analytical text. In the graph structure
encoder, an interactive GNN is employed to construct a communication kernel between atoms
and chemical bonds, enforcing deep dynamic interactions. Experiments conducted on multiple
ADMET benchmark datasets from the TDC platform demonstrate that this method not only
achieves superior performance but also provides explanatory insights for molecular safety

                                                                             2
assessment through visualized reasoning paths..
      (2) A multimodal molecular property prediction framework based on knowledge

enhancement and graph alignment. Addressing the issues of noise redundancy in public medical
texts and the gradient conflicts prone to occur during the joint optimization of multi-source
molecular data, this framework constructs a four-dimensional representation system
encompassing molecular graphs, sequences, fingerprints, and natural language. At the data level,
a knowledge-rewriting pipeline is innovatively designed. This pipeline utilizes an LLM to filter
out redundant noise from original texts and explicitly injects multiple molecular physicochemical
descriptors calculated by the RDKit tool, thereby enhancing the knowledge density and
constraints of the text modality. At the model alignment level, a semantic anchor centered on the
molecular graph is established, and an adaptive weighting mechanism based on homoscedastic
uncertainty is introduced. Systematic evaluations across nine benchmark datasets from
MoleculeNet indicate that this framework achieves strong comprehensive performance in
prediction accuracy, suggesting that graph-centric alignment and knowledge enhancement play a
positive role in performance improvement.
Key words: Molecular Property Prediction, Multimodal Fusion, Chain-of-Thought, Contrastive
Learning
